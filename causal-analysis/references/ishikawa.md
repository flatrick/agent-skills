# Ishikawa or fishbone analysis

Use an Ishikawa analysis to organize candidate or evidence-supported contributing factors around a defined outcome.
Inclusion does not prove causation.

Choose categories that fit the domain.
Common manufacturing labels such as people, method, machine, material, measurement, and environment are prompts, not requirements.
For software or services, components, dependencies, operations, change, data, controls, and environment may fit better.

Give every factor a stable claim or hypothesis ID and status.
Keep unsupported candidates visibly distinct from supported factors.
After brainstorming, test each branch using the causal-claim standard.
Remove irrelevant factors from the final account or retain them as refuted claims with evidence.

Use a fishbone visualization only after opt-in.
Its geometry groups factors; it does not encode causal strength, temporal order, sufficiency, or interaction.
