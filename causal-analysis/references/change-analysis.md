# Change analysis

Use change analysis when a meaningful comparison exists between a good and bad case or before and after an outcome.

Define the comparison pair and why it is relevant.
Inventory differences in what, where, when, extent, environment, people, equipment, software, process, data, and controls.
Verify each difference rather than relying on memory.
Then ask which differences have a mechanism consistent with the outcome and test them against unchanged counterexamples.

A change that precedes the outcome is a candidate, not a cause.
Consider latent conditions revealed by the change, unrelated coincident changes, and failures caused by the absence of an expected change.
Preserve an `unresolved` status when the compared cases differ in several inseparable ways.
