# Five Whys

Use Five Whys for a narrow chain when each answer can be checked against evidence.
Five is a name, not a required count.

1. State one well-bounded outcome.
2. Ask what directly produced or permitted it.
3. Record the answer as a claim, its evidence, and plausible alternatives.
4. Continue only while the next question stays meaningful and evidence can distinguish the answer.
5. Branch when more than one factor matters.
   Stop when the analysis reaches a supported factor that the decision can address, the question leaves scope, or evidence runs out.

Do not accept labels such as "carelessness," "process failure," or "bad culture" without describing an observable mechanism.
Do not force a linear chain when controls, conditions, or concurrent causes interact.
